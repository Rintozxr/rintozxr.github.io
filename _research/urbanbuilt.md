## Overview

How might the physical characteristics of streets shape opportunities for juvenile delinquency?
<br/><br/>
This early research project examined the relationship between urban built environments and juvenile delinquency in Yanping District, Nanping, China. Focusing on three streets: Gulou Street, Sihe Pedestrian Street, and Chaoyang Road. The study explored how spatial configuration, visibility, lighting, and surrounding land uses might influence opportunities for crime.
<br/><br/>

## Approach

The project combined space syntax analysis, field observations, interviews, and street-level spatial surveys to investigate:
<br/><br/>
Spatial configuration: Street connectivity, integration, and visibility.
<br/><br/>
Environmental conditions: Street lighting, enclosure, and surveillance infrastructure.
<br/><br/>
Urban activities: Commercial land uses, nearby schools, and patterns of street use.
<br/><br/>
These analyses informed a set of spatial design proposals, including improvements to sightlines, lighting, pedestrian connectivity, and public facilities.
<br/><br/>

## Reflection
An early exploration of how built environments interact with human behaviour, this project helped establish a research interest that later developed into studies of restorative soundscapes, pedestrian accessibility, and human-centred spatial AI.
