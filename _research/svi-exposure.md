**An interpretable GeoAI framework for deriving human-centered environmental exposure indicators from street-level imagery**

## Overview

This project explores how street-level imagery can be used to model urban environmental exposure in a more interpretable way.

Instead of treating street-view images as black-box visual inputs, the framework represents urban scenes through both **semantic composition** and **spatial structure**. It aims to derive human-centered indicators related to traffic dominance, greenery, visual enclosure, pedestrian space, and other everyday environmental conditions.

---

## Research Focus

Street-level imagery captures the urban environment from a human-scale perspective. However, many image-based approaches reduce scenes to simple visual percentages or use end-to-end prediction models with limited interpretability.

This project asks how visual urban environments can be transformed into indicators that are:

* spatially grounded;
* interpretable;
* scalable across urban areas;
* relevant to human experience, health, and mobility.

---

## Methodological Framework

The workflow includes three main steps:

### 1. Semantic Segmentation

Street-view images are processed to identify visible urban elements such as roads, sidewalks, buildings, vegetation, sky, vehicles, and other environmental features.

### 2. Structural Indicators

Beyond pixel composition, the project explores spatial indicators such as visual dominance, adjacency, continuity, openness, enclosure, and the relationship between traffic space and pedestrian space.

### 3. GIS-Based Interpretation

Image-derived indicators are linked with spatial context, including location, street networks, land use, and neighbourhood morphology.

---

## Contribution

This project contributes to human-centered GeoAI by developing a more transparent way to represent street-level environmental exposure.

It moves beyond asking only *what is visible* in an image, and instead asks how visible environmental elements are spatially arranged and how they may relate to everyday urban experience.

## Current Status

This project is currently under development.

Ongoing work includes:

* testing semantic segmentation workflows;
* developing image-derived spatial indicators;
* linking visual indicators with GIS-based urban context;
* preparing case-study demonstrations.
