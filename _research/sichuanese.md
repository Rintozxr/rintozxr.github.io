## Overview

How can soundscape design connect environmental comfort, cultural identity, and people's experience of urban public space?
<br/>
This undergraduate graduation project proposed a soundscape-oriented landscape design for Shudao Tongqu Park, a 33.2-hectare planned urban park in Chengdu, China.
<br/>
Situated beside a major expressway, the site faced significant traffic noise exposure. At the same time, its location within a developing urban district presented an opportunity to explore how public space could support cultural identity and a sense of belonging among residents from diverse backgrounds.
<br/>
The project used Sichuan dialect as a cultural and auditory theme, exploring how regional language, environmental sound, and spatial design could be brought together in an urban park.

## Approach

The design integrated several strands of analysis and intervention:
<br/>
Sound environment analysis: CADNA/A noise simulation and site assessment to identify areas affected by traffic noise.
<br/>
Soundscape planning: Spatial zoning and landscape interventions, including terrain modification, vegetation, and screening elements, to improve the proposed acoustic environment.

## Reflection
This project marked an early attempt to move beyond visually oriented landscape design and consider how auditory experience shapes people's relationships with urban environments.
<br/>
It also laid the groundwork for my subsequent research into soundscape restoration, human behaviour, and the ways environmental conditions influence spatial experience.
